01 · Overview

Project Overview

Led by Hilton Tnunay at the School of Electrical Engineering and Informatics, Institut Teknologi Bandung, DEEP-UNDER ROV develops an affordable robotic platform and mapping methodology for documenting submerged cultural heritage in Indonesia's shallow tropical waters.

We address three practical constraints in underwater archaeology: risks to human divers, the technical difficulty of collecting reliable data underwater, and the high cost of conventional survey systems. The project brings together robotics, control, computer vision, hydrography, photogrammetry, acoustics, mechanics, and archaeology.

Expected outputs

  • A reusable ROV platform and mapping software
  • Calibrated sensing methods and experimental datasets
  • Geometrically consistent 3D archaeological models
  • International conference and journal publications
  • Practical guidance for lower-cost field mapping

Long-term direction

From a visual-mapping prototype toward a robust multi-modal archaeological survey platform—supporting digital twins, increasingly autonomous missions, and eventually coordinated multi-vehicle surveys of underwater cultural heritage.

02 · Research focus

Annual Research Focus

2025

Platform feasibility

We established the feasibility of a low-cost ROV assembled from components available through the domestic supply chain. The first year integrated underwater cameras and pressure sensing, tested the mechanical, electronic, and sensing systems, developed an initial visual-SLAM pipeline, and validated the platform in controlled and coastal environments.

The trials identified key constraints in illumination, turbidity, vehicle motion, viewing geometry, and sensor integration.

2026

Mapping quality and robustness

The second year shifts from platform feasibility to reliable data acquisition and archaeologically interpretable reconstruction.

01

03 · Progress

Project Milestones

September 2025

01

SUPRI-ROV v1.0 and feasibility testing

The first four-degree-of-freedom prototype established the mechanical frame, propulsion arrangement, onboard electronics, cameras, pressure sensing, and tethered operator workflow. Laboratory and pool tests confirmed that the platform could support underwater inspection and visual-mapping research.

Late 2025

02

Coastal validation and initial visual SLAM

Field validation at Pramuka Island demonstrated stable operation under real underwater pressure and environmental conditions at depths of up to 12 metres. We also demonstrated camera trajectory estimation and sparse 3D reconstruction from underwater imagery.

January 2026

03

SUPRI-ROV v2.0 hardware refinement

The second four-degree-of-freedom iteration refined the enclosure, sealing, component layout, and electrical integration, moving the project from an initial feasibility platform toward a system designed for repeated experiments.

May 2026

04

SUPRI-ROV v3.0 and six-degree-of-freedom operation

The current platform expands motion capability to six degrees of freedom and improves sealing, cable management, power switching, leak detection, fibre-optic communication, and ground-control integration.

June–August 2026

05

Control, estimation, SLAM, and reconstruction

We developed a Gazebo and ROS 2 simulator, stereo-enhanced image-based visual servoing, and a visual-inertial Kalman estimator. We integrated a PINAX underwater camera model into a stereo visual-inertial SLAM pipeline and evaluated it through pool tests at Saraga.

Visual-SLAM and Gaussian-splatting experiments used data from Saraga Pool and Pramuka Island, including reconstruction of an ADCP support frame.

October–November 2026

06

Pramuka Island mission and project completion

The 21–26 October field experiment will test the platform and mapping workflow at Pramuka Island. The programme includes underwater data collection, positioning and reconstruction experiments, and preparation of the final 2026 research outputs.
